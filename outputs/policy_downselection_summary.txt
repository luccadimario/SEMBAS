Safety gates applied (independent of raw pass-rate ranking):
  G1_speed_ceiling: Boundary speed ceiling >= 18.0 mph  (boundary_speed_ceiling >= 18.0)
      Why: 18 mph is the minimum operational speed CARLoS must sustain up to the boundary of its safe envelope to be useful on the target course; a policy that only holds together below this speed is not deployable regardless of how well it scores on pass rate.
  G2_pass_rate: Pass rate >= 35% of sampled test space  (pass_rate >= 0.35)
      Why: 35% is the pass rate of the weakest checkpoint that has historically been considered viable in this project; it is a floor, not a target, so it screens out policies that regressed rather than rewarding the single highest scorer.
  G3_boundary_stability: Boundary drift (std of boundary speed) <= 4.0 mph  (boundary_drift <= 4.0)
      Why: A tight, consistent boundary speed (low std-dev across boundary points) means the failure envelope is predictable; a policy whose boundary speed swings widely is unsafe to certify at any single speed limit even if its mean/max look good.
  G4_near_boundary_safety: Fraction of fail points within 0.15 (normalized) of the boundary <= 0.85  (near_boundary_fail_prob <= 0.85)
      Why: If almost all failures cluster immediately adjacent to the boundary, the safe margin is razor-thin and small perturbations (sensor noise, actuation delay) push the vehicle from pass to fail; capping this fraction ensures some fail points are safely far from the edge, i.e. the envelope has real margin rather than being a knife-edge.
  G5_query_efficiency: Query efficiency (boundary points / n_queries) >= 0.12  (query_efficiency >= 0.12)
      Why: This checks that the boundary search actually found and resolved a reasonably dense boundary given its query budget; low efficiency means the scan under-sampled the boundary and the reported metrics for that checkpoint are statistically less trustworthy.
